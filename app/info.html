What it shows

A live heatmap of wildfire probability over Euskadi. Each point is scored by a Random Forest model, and the area in between is interpolated into a continuous map.

Data sources

  • Euskalmet: real observations from the weather stations for the target day (temperature, humidity, wind, rainfall).
  • Open-Meteo: 30-day weather history at each station, plus a coarse grid covering areas more than 15 km from any station.
  • Model training data: More than 27,957,637 historical weather records from MITECO and the Euskalmet Climate Atlas, combined with historical fire records from public government sources and filtered for Basque Country locations. The training pipeline uses up to 3.5 million prepared records and reserves 20% (about 700,000 records) for testing.

How the map is built

  • Weather features are computed for each station and grid point, including rolling rainfall, dry-spell length, 14-day evapotranspiration and calendar features.
  • The scaler and Random Forest model turn those features into a fire probability.
  • Inverse distance weighting (IDW) interpolates the probabilities between points onto the heatmap.

How the model works

For each day and location, the model uses 18 inputs describing recent weather and the time of year:

  • Temperature and humidity: average, maximum and minimum temperatures, plus average and minimum humidity.
  • Wind and rain: average and maximum wind speed; rainfall over 1, 7, 14 and 30 days; and the length of the current dry spell.
  • Dryness indicators: temperature above or below its recent average, 14-day reference evapotranspiration, a fire-weather indicator, and vapor pressure deficit.
  • Calendar: month and day of the year, so the model can account for seasonal patterns.

The model is a Random Forest made up of 300 decision trees, each with a maximum depth of 18. Each tree applies a series of learned yes-or-no rules to the weather inputs and estimates fire probability from similar historical cases. The model averages the trees' estimates to produce the final probability shown on the map.

Risk tiers

  • Low: P < 0.35 (Safe background weather)
  • Medium: 0.35 ≤ P < 0.50 (Moderate fire weather parameters)
  • High: 0.50 ≤ P < 0.70 (Elevated danger advisory)
  • Extreme: P ≥ 0.70 (Active Fire Risk Alert)

Model performance & metrics

Operational test results on 700,000 held-out samples:

  • Overall model accuracy: 95.40%. Across the test samples, this is the share of predictions the model got right.
  • ROC-AUC: 0.9867. This measures how well the model separates fire cases from non-fire cases; 1.0 is a perfect score.
  • PR-AUC: 0.8062. This summarizes how useful the fire alerts are by considering both correct alerts and how many fire cases are detected.
  • Optimal decision boundary: 0.7000. The model raises an Extreme alert at a predicted probability of 70% or higher. At this setting, 72.83% of alerts matched fire cases (precision), and 72.84% of fire cases were detected (recall).
  • False alarm reduction: 80.5% fewer false alerts at the 0.7000 boundary than at the default 0.50 boundary. A false alert is a high-risk prediction when no fire case occurred.

Limitations

  • The data covers the previous day's daily aggregates, not the current hour.
  • Interpolation between points is approximate and ignores terrain.
  • The map extends slightly beyond Euskadi's borders because it uses a bounding box.
